> "Bless the Lord, O my soul, and forget not all His benefits." — Psalm 103:2

Four years ago I had back surgery. Most back problems don't need surgery. I was one of the "lucky" few who did.

This September I hurt my back again, and it brought me back to this story. It's one of the blessings I never want to forget, so I'm writing it down.

## How it started

Honestly, I'm not sure. My hunch is it built up over time: bad posture and too much sitting, which I think weakened my glutes and tightened my hamstrings. Combine that with explosive sprinting when I exercised or played. My back got strained, and without the right exercises it slowly got worse.

In 2022 I went through PT and painkillers, and I saw orthopaedic doctors in May and July. None of it really fixed it. I kept having back pain at around 6/10.

## The weekend it got bad

On September 17 and 18, 2022, we went on a family trip to Tagaytay. I drove us there from Los Baños, took care of the kids (sometimes carrying them), then drove us back.

When we got home I was tired and my back hurt. Usually I'd just lie down and rest, and the pain would ease. Not this time. On Monday I was in bed the whole day. I couldn't find a good position and I couldn't sleep. Then Tuesday. Then Wednesday. The pain went to a constant 8/10, then 10/10 for several days straight.

## Los Baños Doctors Hospital

Finally I went to Dr. Leslie at Los Baños Doctors Hospital (LBDH), and he decided to admit me (around September 21). They tried a host of painkillers. None really worked, until finally one did. Then, on September 23, I got an epidural shot. You know, the one you get when you give birth. 😐 I never thought I'd get one.

![Me in a hospital bed at LBDH, hooked up to an IV](/assets/blog/my-back-surgery-story/hospital-bed-lbdh.jpg)

![X-ray from the epidural, September 23, 2022](/assets/blog/my-back-surgery-story/epidural-xray.jpg)

After that I felt better, but I knew I still needed surgery. The plan was a laminotomy discectomy on L5-S1, an open back surgery. I asked my doctors how urgent it was, what the risks were, and how comfortable they were with the surgeon and the procedure.

Honestly, I wasn't really knowledgeable about my options. I would have gone with the open back surgery.

## A call from Sorsogon

Then my Tito Roland (Dr. Roland Hermo) from Sorsogon contacted me. He said that if I was open to doing it in Sorsogon, at the Sorsogon Medical Mission Group Hospital (SMMGH), he recommended Dr. Elmer Maceda and a minimally invasive approach: endoscopic spine surgery.

It uses a small incision and specialized instruments to remove the parts of the slipped disc that were pinching my nerves at L5-S1. Less tissue trauma and a quicker recovery than open back surgery.

To be honest, this was God-sent.

## Surgery week

- **October 2:** Darla, Caleb (still a baby then) and I flew to Sorsogon.
- **October 4:** surgery.
- **October 5:** I was up and walking well.
- **October 8:** we flew back home.

![The endoscope's view during surgery](/assets/blog/my-back-surgery-story/endoscope-view.jpg)

![A piece of the slipped disc they took out](/assets/blog/my-back-surgery-story/disc-fragment.jpg)

Two weeks earlier I couldn't find a position to sleep in. A day after surgery, I was walking.

![Thumbs up, standing in my hospital room the day after surgery](/assets/blog/my-back-surgery-story/walking-day-after.jpg)

## Back to an active life

To be honest, the recovery was almost instant. A day after surgery I was walking okay, and I could already work. I took it easy for about 6 weeks, just walking, before doing any other exercise.

After that I got into basketball, then into tennis.

Two years later, on my 37th birthday, I played tennis in the morning and basketball in the evening. I love physical activity, and I'm grateful to God for restoring my health and letting me enjoy an active life again.

## What I'd tell you

- **Know your options.** Ask whether a minimally invasive approach fits your case. I almost went with open surgery simply because I didn't know there was another way.
- **Ask your doctors questions.** How urgent is it? What are the risks? How experienced is the surgeon with this exact procedure?
- **Surgery fixes the disc, not why it happened.** I stayed active after surgery and kept a healthy lifestyle. Still, this September, playing on a tired body, I hurt my back again. More on that in [My Low Back Ability journey](/posts/my-low-back-ability-journey).

Praise God, He was with us all through it. He provided for us, and He healed me fully.
