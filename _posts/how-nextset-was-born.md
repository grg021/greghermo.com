Four years ago, I had [surgery on my lower back](/posts/my-back-surgery-story).

Two months ago, I found myself dealing with lower back pain again, this time probably from pushing myself too hard with tennis. 🙈

Somewhere between recovering, getting back on the court, and figuring out how to manage my training, I ended up building a workout app.

Here's the story of how NextSet came to life.

## I've always loved working out

Some of my earliest memories of fitness go back to when I was a kid. I'd wake up around 4:30 AM to my dad getting ready to jog, or someone at home following a workout video.

Remember Billy Blanks and Tae Bo? Anyone? 🙋‍♂️🙋‍♀️

I'd join in whenever I could. I was also pretty active growing up, always running around or playing sports at school.

From grade school to high school, I played table tennis. In college, I tried basketball and spent a few months going to the gym (though, to be honest, I probably spent more time playing DOTA 😅).

After college, I got into running, cycling, and bodyweight training. I've always enjoyed working out at home or outdoors.

![Running the 21K at the Great UP Run (photo by CN Creatives)](/assets/blog/how-nextset-was-born/great-up-run-21k.jpg)

## Then came a major setback

About four years ago, I underwent surgery for my lower back. Praise God, I recovered well and eventually returned to an active lifestyle.

![Me in a hospital bed, hooked up to an IV](/assets/blog/my-back-surgery-story/hospital-bed-lbdh.jpg)

These days, tennis has become my main sport. But I also make it a point to take care of my back and keep it strong.

![Tennis with the family](/assets/blog/how-nextset-was-born/family-tennis.jpg)

Then, about two months ago, I had another scare: lower back pain. I was probably playing too much tennis without giving my body enough recovery. I kept pushing until my body couldn't keep up.

That's when I discovered **LBA (Low Back Ability)**, a workout program and community focused on recovering, rebuilding, and strengthening your lower back. (I wrote about it in [my Low Back Ability journey](/posts/my-low-back-ability-journey).)

I was able to return to tennis after about three weeks, but I continued training my back.

![After a night match](/assets/blog/how-nextset-was-born/tennis-match-night.jpg)

![After a day match](/assets/blog/how-nextset-was-born/tennis-match-day.jpg)

This brought up a challenge: how do I manage workouts that need to adapt to my back recovery, tennis sessions, tournaments, and everyday life?

I was already using Grokbot to track my LBA progress, research workouts, and keep track of my match calendar. I wanted a workout app that could communicate with my AI assistant.

So I started building one.

**That's how NextSet was born.**

One feature I'm particularly excited about is the ability to create routines that go beyond regular exercises.

Take a tennis pre-match routine, for example. Preparing for a match isn't just physical; it's mental, too. You might need mobility work, activation exercises, breathing, or meditation. NextSet gives me the flexibility to put all of these together into one routine.

Darla and I have been using it, testing it, tweaking it, and adding features along the way.

## So, what exactly is NextSet?

NextSet is a workout app for iPhone and Android that helps you track your workouts and progress, with ready-made training programs to get you started.

But what makes it different is that it can connect with AI assistants like Claude, ChatGPT, and Grok through their plugin and connector integrations.

![NextSet: tell your AI, your workout updates](/assets/blog/how-nextset-was-born/nextset-app.webp)

This means your training doesn't have to be locked into a fixed workout plan. You can work with your AI assistant to make your training more dynamic and adapt it around your goals, schedule, sport, and whatever life throws at you.

I started working on NextSet just three weeks ago, and it's still in beta. There's a lot more to build, and I'm excited to see where it goes.

## Building in public

Now, here's another part of this journey I'm looking forward to sharing.

I've been developing apps for around 18 years now (I think... 😄). I've always loved the building part: solving problems, writing code, and turning ideas into something people can actually use.

But as any app developer will probably tell you, building the product is only half the story. The other half is getting it in front of the people who might actually need it.

Branding and marketing have always felt like a different ball game to me. It's something I'm still learning, and honestly, it can be pretty daunting.

So I'm taking this as an opportunity to learn, experiment, and build in public. I'll be sharing the development process, features, challenges, lessons, and hopefully some wins along the way.

And of course, a little support from friends and family would mean a lot!
